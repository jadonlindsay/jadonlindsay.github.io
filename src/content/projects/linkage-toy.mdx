## The Brief

Design a toy driven by an approved linkage: one fixed point and one free end that moves. Then take it from a 2D mechanism to a 3D, gear-driven product.

## The Concept

I studied videos of the approved linkages and looked at the path each one's free end traced. The Chebyshev lambda linkage's path reminded me of a kite bobbing in the wind, so I proposed a character built around that. The group refined it into Russell from *Up* holding a balloon: the fixed point is his hand, and the free end is the balloon drifting in the wind.

## My Role

I owned the CAD: modeling the linkage parts and building the full SOLIDWORKS assembly, which was the large majority of the modeling work. My teammates led the physical assembly and painting.

## Process

1. **Concept sketches:** hand drawings with dimensions and front, side, and bottom views
2. **2D prototype:** parts modeled in SOLIDWORKS, a color-coded assembly to verify motion, then laser-cut wood joined with wood glue and nails as pins
3. **3D gear-driven version:** a hand crank drives a pair of 3D-printed bevel gears that transfer motion between planes, so the crank and the linkage don't have to sit in the same plane. Built from laser-cut wood and PLA parts, then painted.

## The Problem: Joint Play

In the 2D prototype, the nail pins were slightly smaller than their holes, so the joints had too much play and the motion was sloppy. For the 3D version we upsized the pins to fit the holes properly, tightening up the motion.

## Result

The final toy moved as intended and was presented at the Toy Fair, where it stood out for its finish; only about 2 other groups out of 30 painted theirs.
